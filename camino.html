What is the Camino?

The Camino de Santiago (the "Way of St. James") is a network of ancient pilgrimage routes across Spain that all lead to one place: the cathedral in Santiago de Compostela, Spain, where tradition holds that the remains of the apostle James are buried. Over 500,000 people walked the Camino last year, and it continues to grow in popularity and international influence.

Pilgrims from all over the world walk for several days to several weeks. They walk over mountains, across rivers, through forests, on cobblestones, gravel, and highways, into small villages and large cities all to end in Santiago de Compostela. They encounter physical and mental challenges while enjoying deep social comradery and the culture and beauty of Spain.

Evangelism on the Camino

The Camino provides an amazing opportunity for evangelism.

People have walked these paths for over a thousand years — originally for purely religious reasons, to make themselves right before God through their own effort. Today, motivations to walk the Camino range from desiring spiritual connection, healing after a loss, having space to process, discovering themselves, pursuing adventure, challenging themselves, etc. No matter their reason for walking, pilgrims are searching for answers and purpose. They have very little to distract them and plenty of time for conversation and processing plus they are wanting to think through their life. We get to meet them along the way in their search and help point them to Jesus as the answer.

The pilgrim center provides an excellent place to connect with these pilgrims, as it lies on one of the most popular routes. Our vision for the pilgrim center is that it would be a place where pilgrims are welcomed in, shown hospitality, and helped on their journey towards Christ.

The Impact of the Camino
